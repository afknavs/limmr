04 / The pitch

How to pitch it.

“Limmr gives your AIs the WhatsApp context you choose, organized around people and shared work, with permissions you can inspect.”

The problem

Plans, assignments, decisions and requests live in WhatsApp. An agent outside those conversations starts without context. Copying threads by hand loses relationships and provenance; sharing an entire inbox gives away too much.

Who it is for

A Mac user already working with AI clients who wants help across several circles: a student coordinating a project, a freelancer following client work, or a small team juggling plans. Each needs context across chats and a clear boundary around unrelated life.

The promise

Let a chosen AI catch up, understand the people involved and maintain a useful brief. Keep the owner in control of visibility, send rules and the record of what happened.

People, not just search

Circles, profiles, facts, briefs and efforts give an agent a durable map of what a conversation means.

One engine, several clients

MCP lets permitted clients use the same context and update it with attribution instead of building disconnected memories.

Local and inspectable

The owner sees access, hidden chats, activity and waiting work in a native Mac app. Remote access is a deliberate extra step.

Permission is part of the product

Each AI gets a profile, circle access and an optional expiry. Send rules can require confirmation or block sending.

Product position, not a claim of proven market demand. This is a local Mac app with an unofficial WhatsApp connection.

This is the product thesis. The documents do not establish adoption, revenue, market size, exclusive technology or a competitive benchmark.

Use invented sample data throughout. Prepare a Work-scoped integration, a named effort and a waiting draft. Demonstrate the prepared outcome without sending a WhatsApp message.

The catch-up result is a clearly labeled prepared sample, not a live model call. A sample draft is a presentation state, not evidence of delivered messaging.

AI tools are becoming places to do ongoing work

Once an assistant helps with more than a single answer, it needs continuity: the people, prior decisions, constraints and open asks behind a request. A connector can supply that context through a shared protocol rather than a separate inbox for each client.

The limits belong in the demo.

Say this clearlyWhat it means
Unofficial WhatsApp clientNo claim of WhatsApp endorsement or account safety. Reliability and account-policy risk remain.
Mac stays part of the systemThe engine is a local helper. Remote clients need the Mac, a configured HTTPS endpoint and approved OAuth access.
Local-first is about storage and controlA cloud AI still receives permitted content. Access expiry and revocation stop future reads; they cannot erase prior copies.
Not every send needs a Mac approvalSend freely is a trust choice. Confirm in chat enforces two steps, not proof of a human click. Never is available for stricter boundaries.
Efforts are inferredGrouping and names can be wrong; evidence and naming credit help review. Confidence is not a calibrated probability.
Performance is workload-dependentKeep historical speedups separate from current startup behavior. P9 records a remaining cold-Home problem.
Demo proof has a boundarySample captures and synthetic tests do not prove live delivery, physical accessibility or installed updater recovery.

A credible pitch makes the permission choices and operational limits easy to understand. It does not hide them behind the final slide.

Messaging context needs a boundary

WhatsApp mixes work and personal life. The useful unit is often a circle of people and chats, with shared work spanning several conversations. A permissioned people layer gives those relationships an explicit model.

Three rooms. The same promise.

To a friend / about 20 seconds

“Most of my plans are in WhatsApp, but my AI has no idea who anyone is. Limmr lets me share a chosen circle, keeps a useful brief, and shows what the AI read or wanted to do. I can keep private chats hidden.”

To a hackathon judge / about 30 seconds

“Limmr is a native Mac app with a Go engine that exposes permissioned WhatsApp context through MCP. Its people layer connects profiles, circles, briefs and ongoing efforts. Each AI has its own access and send rules, and the owner can inspect activity. Our demo uses invented data and shows both the useful context and the access boundary.”

To an investor / about 30 seconds

“Personal AI becomes more useful when it understands the relationships behind a request. Limmr starts with WhatsApp: a local, permissioned people layer that several AI clients can share. The wedge is users already coordinating real work in chats. The thesis is that maintained context and visible control earn repeated use. We have a working local product; retention, willingness to pay, distribution and platform risk still need validation.”

Use only claims supported by the current build. Keep prepared samples labeled; do not present the historical performance milestones as a service-level promise.
